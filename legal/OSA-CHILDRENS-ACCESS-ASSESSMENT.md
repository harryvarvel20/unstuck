> Every user-to-user and search service must assess whether children can
> access it, and if so whether a significant number do or whether it is likely
> to attract them. This is that record.

---

## Stage 1 — Is it possible for children to access the service?

**Assessment: yes, technically possible — but restricted.**

ADHV is a public website. There is no hard age verification at the point of
viewing the marketing pages, and a determined under-18 could enter an email
address. We therefore answer "yes" and proceed to Stage 2 rather than claiming
an exemption.

**Access restrictions actually in place:**

| Layer                    | Control                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Signing up               | Account requires an email magic link; the sign-in screen states you must be **18 or over** and agree to the Terms                                           |
| Terms                    | Explicitly restrict the service to 18+; we close accounts we learn belong to under-18s                                                                      |
| Social features          | Behind a **paid subscription** — requires a payment card, a practical barrier for most minors                                                               |
| Public sharing           | Additionally requires an explicit **"I'm 18 or over"** confirmation; without it, any attempt to post publicly is **downgraded to friends-only server-side** |
| Children in Parents Mode | Are **not users at all** — no child login, no child account, no child data. They only ever see a shared screen the parent is holding                        |

## Stage 2 — Is a significant number of children using it, or is it likely to attract them?

**Assessment: no, on both limbs.**

**(a) Significant number of child users — no.**
Pre-launch, single-figure user base, all known adults. There is no evidence or
indication of child users. We will monitor (see "Monitoring" below).

**(b) Likely to attract children — no.** Reasoning against each of the usual
attraction factors:

| Factor that attracts children  | ADHV                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Subject matter                 | Adult self-management: tax returns, admin, work tasks, household chores, parenting _as the parent_, burnout                                                          |
| Design and tone                | Deliberately restrained "Old Money" aesthetic — ivory, navy, aged gold, editorial serif; no bright colours, cartoons, mascots, characters, or child-oriented imagery |
| Content format                 | Text-led, no video, no short-form feed, no music, no filters, no avatars, no games                                                                                   |
| Social mechanics children seek | **None present:** no follower counts, no likes to chase, no leaderboards, no streaks, no virality, no stranger discovery, no group chat, no live features            |
| Marketing                      | Targeted at adults with ADHD and parents of children with ADHD — the _parents_, never the children                                                                   |
| Commercial model               | Paid subscription; free tier is limited and not gamified                                                                                                             |
| Influencer/celebrity appeal    | Creator partnerships (if any) are in the adult ADHD space                                                                                                            |

The only child-adjacent surface is **Parents Mode**, and it is explicitly a
tool an adult operates _beside_ a child on a shared screen. The child is not a
user, holds no account, is not tracked, and no data about them is collected —
so it does not make children "users of the service".

**Conclusion: ADHV is not likely to be accessed by children, and the
children's risk assessment duties for child users are not triggered.**

## Stage 3 — Consequences and continuing duties

Because the answer at Stage 2 is "no", the additional children's safety duties
do not apply. Nevertheless we maintain the following, because they are the
right thing to do and because they are the evidence that Stage 2 remains true:

1. **18+ enforced** at signup and before any public sharing (server-side).
2. **Terms and Privacy** state the 18+ requirement and commit us to closing
   under-18 accounts we become aware of.
3. **Children's Code standards honoured on kid-facing shared screens anyway:**
   no data collection, no profiling, no nudge techniques, no behavioural
   advertising, no third-party trackers, no external links, no analytics
   events whatsoever.
4. **Suicide/self-harm and safeguarding content** is intercepted by
   deterministic gates before publication, on every free-text surface, for all
   users regardless of age, and is never paywalled.
5. **No pornography, no gambling, no age-restricted commercial content** is
   present on the service at all.

## Monitoring — what would change this conclusion

We will re-run this assessment promptly if any of the following occurs:

- Evidence emerges of under-18s registering (e.g. reports, support emails,
  or age signals in feedback).
- The product adds child-appealing features: avatars, games, video, streaks,
  leaderboards, short-form feeds, group chat, or stranger discovery.
- Marketing shifts toward children or toward platforms with predominantly
  child audiences.
- A child account is ever permitted in Parents Mode (currently impossible by
  design and would require re-doing the DPIA as well).

Otherwise reviewed at least annually.
